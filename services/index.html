CHOOSING

Which one is right for you?

A short version of the conversation I have with almost every client. Find the row that sounds like you.

Choose WordPress

  • You need real ecommerce or deep third-party integrations
  • You have a growing content operation
  • You want full control over hosting and your data

The most capable of the three — and the maintenance that comes with it is what a retainer covers.

Choose Webflow

  • You want design precision without a developer in the loop
  • Your marketing team needs to publish on its own
  • You have no server you want to own

Less flexible at the edges than WordPress, and almost nothing to maintain.

Choose a custom frontend

  • The site is really an application
  • It is part of a product with its own API and design system
  • It has to fit a codebase you already run

React or Nuxt, built to match what your team already maintains.

SEO is not an either/or

It is not an alternative to the three above — it is what makes whichever you pick actually pay off. Worth scoping separately when:

  • An existing site is not being found
  • A redesign risks the rankings you already have
  • You want the technical foundations right before spending anything on content

None of this stays fixed on its own

WordPress in particular needs upkeep after launch — updates, backups, monitoring — or it drifts back toward the same problems this page just walked through. That's what a WordPress Maintenance plan covers.

Still unsure? Describe what the site has to do and who will maintain it, and I will tell you which of these fits — including when the answer is that you do not need me at all.

HOW IT RUNS

The same process either way

WordPress, Webflow or a custom front end — the stages do not change. Only the tooling does.

  1. Discovery and a fixed quote

    What the site has to achieve, who it is for, what is failing now. You get a written scope and a fixed price before anything starts — see what actually drives that price by platform, or see starting prices.

  2. Structure before design

    Page architecture, URLs, heading hierarchy and keyword targets first. Skipping this is the most common reason a good-looking site never ranks.

  3. Build

    Mobile-first, accurate to the design, tested at real widths on real devices rather than in a builder preview.

  4. Performance, SEO and accessibility

    A dedicated stage with its own checklist — Core Web Vitals, structured data, image compression, keyboard navigation, contrast.

  5. Launch and handover

    Redirect mapping so existing rankings survive, Search Console and analytics setup, and a recorded walkthrough so your team can run it.
