THE PROBLEM

Why a WordPress site needs upkeep at all

WordPress, its plugins and its theme are separately maintained software running together, and none of them stand still. A site that isn’t looked after doesn’t stay in its launch-day state — it drifts further from a tested, compatible combination every month an update is skipped or applied blind, until an ordinary update becomes the thing that breaks it.

WHAT USUALLY GOES WRONG WITHOUT IT

  • Updates applied straight to the live site, with no staging test, until one of them breaks something
  • A known plugin vulnerability sitting unpatched for months because nobody was watching for it
  • No recent backup when something does go wrong, or a backup nobody ever tested actually restores
  • Downtime discovered by a customer or by Google, not by the site owner
  • A different freelancer or agency starting from scratch every time something breaks, re-learning the site each time
  • Small requests — a text change, a broken link — piling up because there's no standing arrangement to just get them done

PROCESS

How the monthly cycle runs

  1. Onboarding audit

    Before the first cycle starts, the site gets a full health check — outdated plugins, security gaps, backup status — so nothing inherited stays hidden.

  2. Staging environment set up

    A private copy of the live site is created specifically for testing updates before they touch production.

  3. Updates tested, then applied

    Core, plugin and theme updates run on staging first; anything that breaks something gets caught there instead of on the live site.

  4. Backup taken before every update round

    A fresh restore point exists immediately before any change, independent of the regular daily backup schedule.

  5. Security and uptime monitored continuously

    Not just checked once a month — ongoing monitoring flags problems as they happen, not at the next scheduled visit.

  6. Included hours used on request

    Small changes get done as they come in, tracked against the plan's included time rather than batched into a big backlog.

  7. Monthly report delivered

    What was updated, what was found, what was fixed — in writing every month, plus a call on the Priority plan.

FAQ

Questions I get asked

What people ask before signing up for a plan.

What actually happens to my site every month?

Core, plugin and theme updates get applied on every plan, but tested on a staging copy first rather than pushed straight to the live site — which is the specific step that prevents the "update broke my site" problem. Backups run daily, uptime is monitored continuously, and higher tiers add security scanning and hands-on time for small changes.

What counts as a “small change” in the included hours?

Text edits, image swaps, a new page from existing components, a plugin setting, a broken link fix — the kind of request that takes minutes to an hour, not a new feature build. Anything larger gets scoped and quoted separately, same as any other project.

What happens if something breaks between monthly visits?

Uptime monitoring alerts as soon as the site goes down, not at the next scheduled check-in, and a fix starts immediately rather than waiting for a monthly cycle. Growth and Priority plans include faster response commitments for exactly this.

Is this instead of your one-off Bug Fix or Malware Removal services?

It's meant to prevent needing them as often, not replace them entirely — an active infection or a fatal error already in progress still gets handled as a one-off, billed separately or covered by included hours depending on the plan and scope. What a maintenance plan actually reduces is how often those calls happen in the first place.

Can I cancel if it isn't working out?

Yes, month to month, no long-term contract. You keep whatever updates and hardening were already applied; there's nothing to undo on cancellation.

Do you need admin access to my site to do this?

Yes, WordPress admin access at minimum, and hosting or FTP access for backups and server-level checks where relevant. Access can be limited to a dedicated maintenance user account rather than your own personal login.

Which plan is right if I only have one small site?

Essential covers the core risk — untested updates breaking the site — for the lowest ongoing cost. Growth or Priority make more sense once the site is revenue-generating (a store, a lead source) where downtime or a slow security response has a real cost attached.
