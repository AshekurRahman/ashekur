THE PROBLEM

Why is my WooCommerce checkout losing orders?

WooCommerce is not one system, it is WordPress plus a payment gateway plus a shipping and tax rule engine, usually with a caching layer sitting on top of all three. Checkout is the one moment every part of that chain has to work correctly at the same time, which is exactly why it is the most common place things quietly break. Not sure yet if that is what is happening to you? The free WooCommerce Store Health Checker gives an instant, cause-first read on checkout caching, HTTPS and payment gateway detection before you send anything over.

WHAT USUALLY CAUSES IT

  • A payment gateway API key, webhook URL or SSL certificate is misconfigured or has expired
  • A caching or speed plugin is caching the cart or checkout page and serving stale session data
  • Two plugins are both hooking into WooCommerce’s checkout process and conflicting
  • Shipping zones, classes or tax rules are configured differently than what you actually intend to charge
  • A WooCommerce, gateway or WordPress core update shipped without your theme or a custom snippet being retested against it
  • Stock, variation or inventory data has fallen out of sync with a connected channel or plugin

SIGNS YOU NEED THIS

The WooCommerce problems that actually lose sales

A broken storefront costs money every hour it stays broken. These are the issues I get called in for most, roughly in order of urgency.

Checkout not completing, or payment errors

Orders failing at the last step are the most expensive bug on any store. Usually a gateway configuration, SSL, or plugin conflict at the point of payment. I test with real transactions, not just the sandbox.

Shipping or tax calculating wrong

Wrong rates, zones not applying, or tax miscalculating at checkout. I audit the rule configuration rather than just resetting it and hoping.

Products, stock or variations not displaying correctly

Variations missing, stock showing available when it is not, or products not appearing in the catalog. Usually a sync, cache, or attribute configuration issue.

Site slows down or crashes under real traffic

A store that works fine in testing but buckles during a sale or a traffic spike. I look at hosting, caching and plugin load together, not just one of them.

PROCESS

How a fix runs

  1. Diagnose what’s failing

    Which step breaks, for which customers, and since when. A failed order or a screenshot of the error is enough to start.

  2. Check the logs

    WooCommerce’s own status logs, the gateway’s transaction log and the PHP error log get read together, since a checkout failure usually leaves a trace in more than one of them.

  3. Identify the root cause

    I reproduce the issue on a staging copy or with test transactions, isolating whether it is the gateway, a plugin conflict or a configuration setting, so live orders are never put at risk during diagnosis.

  4. Create a safe backup

    A full backup of the store database and files is taken before anything changes, since checkout and payment settings are not somewhere to guess.

  5. Apply the fix

    The confirmed cause gets corrected — a gateway setting, a rule, a conflicting hook — on staging first.

  6. Test with real transactions

    The fix is tested against actual checkout flows, not just the sandbox, including the specific payment methods your customers use.

  7. Check mobile checkout

    Most storefront traffic checks out on a phone, so the cart and checkout flow get tested on mobile specifically, not just confirmed on desktop.

  8. Verify performance and security

    SSL, API keys and any exposed endpoints get checked, along with a look at whether the fix added any drag to page load at checkout.

  9. Final quality check and monitoring

    Deployed during a safe window with a rollback point taken first, then watched through at least one full order cycle to confirm it holds under real use.

FAQ

Questions I get asked

What people ask when a store stops taking orders.

My checkout is losing orders. Can you find out why?

Yes, this is one of the most common jobs I get. I check the gateway configuration, SSL, plugin conflicts and the checkout flow itself, then test with real transactions to confirm the fix actually works.

Do you work with Stripe, PayPal and other gateways?

Yes, and most of the major WooCommerce payment gateways. If you use a less common regional gateway, tell me which one and I will confirm before starting.

Can you fix shipping and tax calculation errors?

Yes. These are usually a rule configuration issue rather than a bug in WooCommerce itself, and I audit the actual rules against what you intend to charge.

Is this covered if my store is about to go into a sale or traffic spike?

Yes, and if you have a specific date coming up, tell me early. Fixing an issue before a major traffic spike is a different priority than fixing it after.

Do you offer ongoing WooCommerce maintenance?

Yes, core, plugin and gateway updates, plus monitoring, on a monthly arrangement. Plenty of store owners bring me in for the initial fix and then handle updates in-house afterward. See WordPress Maintenance for plans and pricing.

What if I don’t know exactly what’s wrong, just that something is off?

That is fine, most people who contact me are in that position. Describe what you have noticed and I will run a proper diagnostic to find the actual cause.

Will fixing this affect my existing orders or customer data?

No — a fix targets the specific broken function, such as a gateway, a shipping rule, or a plugin conflict, not your orders or customer records. I also test on a staging copy first specifically so live order data is never at risk.

Is there a fixed price for a WooCommerce fix?

Small, well-defined issues are usually quoted as a fixed price once I’ve seen the error. Unclear or intermittent issues start with a paid diagnostic so neither of us is guessing at scope. See current starting prices for a reference point.
