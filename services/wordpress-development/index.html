THE PROBLEM

What I actually get called in to fix

WordPress runs a large share of the web for good reason, but the same flexibility that makes it powerful is what makes so many WordPress sites slow, fragile and impossible to edit. Most of the work I am asked to do is not building from nothing — it is fixing a site that grew that way. I have been doing this since 2015, and the goal is always the same: a site your team can run, that does not get slower every time someone adds a page.

The site takes too long to load

Stacked page-builder markup, uncompressed images and a dozen overlapping plugins turn a simple brochure site into a slow one. Visitors leave before the hero image finishes, and Google notices. I audit what is actually on the page, remove what is not earning its place, and rebuild the critical rendering path.

Every small change needs a developer

If updating a phone number means raising a support ticket, the site quietly stops being maintained. I build editable structures your team can genuinely use — proper content fields and templates that hold their layout no matter what gets typed into them.

It does not show up in search

Missing page titles, duplicated meta descriptions, no heading hierarchy, pages Google has never indexed. These are unglamorous problems with a direct revenue cost. I fix the technical layer and make sure each page has a clear reason to rank for something specific.

It works on desktop and falls apart on a phone

Most traffic is mobile, and the page-builder preview is not a real device. I build mobile-first and test at real widths, so the layout that most of your visitors actually see is the one that got the attention.

PROCESS

How a project runs

  1. Discovery and scope

    We start with what the site has to achieve, who it is for, and what is failing right now. If there is an existing site, I audit it first. You get a written scope and a fixed quote before anything is built, so there are no surprises in either direction — the same basics worth checking for in any WordPress freelancer you hire.

  2. Structure and content map

    Page architecture, URL structure, heading hierarchy and keyword targets are decided before design starts. This is the step most projects skip, and it is the one that determines whether the site can rank at all.

  3. Design and build

    Custom theme work, responsive from the smallest width upward. If you have a Figma file I build to it precisely; if you do not, I work from the structure we agreed and keep it clean and conventional.

  4. Performance, SEO and accessibility pass

    Core Web Vitals, structured data, meta titles and descriptions, image compression and alt text, keyboard navigation and colour contrast. This is a dedicated stage, not something squeezed in at the end.

  5. Launch and handover

    Migration, redirect mapping so existing rankings survive, Search Console and analytics setup, then a walkthrough recorded for your team so they can run the site themselves.

FAQ

Questions I get asked

What people ask before starting a WordPress project, answered up front rather than in the first call.

How much does a WordPress website cost?

It depends entirely on scope, and I would rather quote accurately than advertise a number that turns out to be wrong. A focused brochure site is a very different piece of work from a WooCommerce storefront with product variations and payment integration. Tell me what you need and roughly when you need it, and you will get a fixed written quote before any work begins. See current starting prices for a reference point.

How long does a WordPress project take?

A straightforward business site is usually a matter of weeks rather than months, provided content is ready. The single biggest cause of delay on every project I have run is waiting for copy and images, so if you want it moving quickly, that is the part to prepare early.

Do you build with Elementor or custom themes?

Both, and the right answer depends on who will maintain the site. Elementor makes sense when a non-technical team needs to build new pages themselves. A custom theme makes sense when performance and precision matter more than in-house page building. I will tell you honestly which one fits your situation rather than defaulting to whichever is faster for me.

Can you fix or take over an existing WordPress site?

Yes, and a good share of my work is exactly that. I start with an audit covering performance, security, SEO and code quality, then give you a prioritised list of what is worth fixing and what is not. Sometimes the honest answer is that a rebuild costs less than the repairs, and I will say so.

Will I be able to update the site myself?

That is the intended outcome. Content areas are built as proper editable fields with layouts that hold no matter what you put in them, and every project ends with a recorded walkthrough for your team.

Do you offer ongoing maintenance?

Yes — core and plugin updates, backups, security hardening and uptime monitoring, on a monthly arrangement. It is optional, and plenty of clients handle it in-house after handover. See WordPress Maintenance for plans and pricing.

Do you work with clients outside Bangladesh?

Most of my clients are outside Bangladesh — the UK, Germany and the US primarily. I am based in Bangladesh and work across European and North American time zones without difficulty, with a same-day reply to anything urgent.

Should I hire a freelancer or an agency for a WordPress site?

For most single-site WordPress projects, an experienced freelancer covers the work end to end for less than an agency's rate, with a shorter chain between you and whoever is actually writing the code. An agency earns its overhead on projects that genuinely need several people working in parallel against a hard deadline. I have worked both sides — see the full freelancer-vs-agency comparison if you want to weigh it properly before deciding.
