The ask

A PSD from Quomodosoft, to become a single-product landing page good enough to sell on ThemeForest.

The build

Converted by hand into HTML, CSS and JavaScript — specs, gallery, video and a cart, all on one scroll.

The outcome

Submitted to ThemeForest and approved for sale.

THE PROBLEM

One product, one scroll, and a reviewer first

The design arrived as a Photoshop file, and the destination was ThemeForest — where submissions are reviewed against a published standard and a great many are turned away on code quality, responsive behaviour and browser support rather than on looks. The page itself has a single job a catalogue never has: carry the entire argument for buying and then close, without the visitor navigating away. Neither of those is settled by a flat comp.

THE SOLUTION

Converted in the order a buyer decides

I converted the PSD into HTML, CSS and JavaScript by hand, running the argument in the order it gets made: what the product is, what it does, what is inside it, what it looks like in use, and what it costs — with the cart in reach the whole way down. Everything a buyer of the template would want to change is a block they can swap rather than a layout they have to unpick.

  • Anchor navigation across features, specification, how-to-use and price — no page loads
  • Feature blocks for battery, weight and connectivity, each with its own visual
  • A specification table and a video walkthrough, for the buyer who reads before deciding
  • Colour variants priced and purchasable, with a running cart and subtotal

THE STACK

Chosen for who picks it up next

Static HTML on Bootstrap with jQuery for behaviour, carousels for the product imagery and a lightbox for the video — the stack a template buyer expects to open and recognise, rather than one that makes them learn a build step first:

  • HTML5
  • CSS3
  • Bootstrap
  • jQuery
  • Swiper
  • Owl Carousel
  • Magnific Popup
  • WOW.js

THE RESULT

Approved on ThemeForest

It passed submission and went on sale. As with the agency template before it, the approval is the part that carries weight outside a portfolio: an unaffiliated reviewer checking the build against a standard, with no reason to be generous about it.
