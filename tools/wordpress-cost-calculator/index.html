How this estimate is calculated

The starting point is the same published price on the pricing page — $1,299 for WordPress, $1,999 for Webflow. From there, each answer adds a modest, clearly-labeled amount for the scope it represents: more pages, a fully custom design instead of a theme, ecommerce complexity, integrations, content migration and optional SEO foundations. The result is a range, not a single number, because a range is honest and a single number rarely survives contact with a real project.

Why "fix or troubleshoot" doesn't get a price here

A hacked site, a broken plugin conflict and a slow checkout are three completely different jobs with no shared scope a calculator can estimate honestly. That work is priced the same way it always is on this site — a quick look at what's actually wrong, then a fixed quote before anything starts. The calculator routes you straight to whichever service page matches instead of guessing at a number.

What this estimate doesn't cover

Ongoing hosting, domain renewal and any third-party subscription costs (a booking plugin, a premium theme licence) sit outside a build price and vary by project. Frontend application work (React, Next.js, Vue/Nuxt) and technical SEO scope also aren't covered here — both are priced individually after discovery, the same as they are on the pricing page, because they vary too widely for a general estimate to mean anything.

FAQ

Questions about this calculator

Is this calculator's estimate a real quote?

No, and it says so throughout — it's a starting point for budgeting, the same honest framing used on the pricing page. Cost depends on scope, design complexity, integrations and technical complexity that a six-question calculator can't fully capture. You get a written, fixed quote before any work begins.

Does it store or send my answers anywhere?

No. The whole calculator runs in your browser — there's no server request, no database, no tracking of your specific answers. If you click through to start a project, your answers are passed along in the link purely so the enquiry already has context; nothing is saved before that.

Why doesn't it give a price for fixing or troubleshooting a site?

Because there's no honest range to show. A hacked site, a broken plugin, or a slow page all have wildly different scopes depending on what's actually wrong — that gets priced after a quick look, not from a page-count questionnaire.
