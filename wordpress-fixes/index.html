HOW TO USE THIS

Find the symptom, not the guess

Most WordPress advice starts from a cause — “how to fix a plugin conflict” — when what you actually have is a symptom: a blank page, a spinning editor, a checkout that silently stopped working. Each guide below starts from what you’re actually seeing on screen, explains why it happens, and walks through diagnosing and fixing it before it tells you when it’s worth handing over to someone else.

FAQ

About this library

My problem isn’t listed here yet — what should I do?

This library is being built out guide by guide, so not every WordPress problem has a page yet. Send me a description of what’s happening (or a screenshot of the error) through the contact page and I’ll tell you directly what’s likely going on — you don’t need a matching article to reach out.

Are these guides written for developers or regular website owners?

Website owners first. Each guide explains what’s actually happening in plain language, then gives the technical steps for anyone comfortable with FTP or wp-admin. If a step needs a developer, the guide says so rather than assuming you’ll manage it.

Do I have to pay to use these fixes?

No. Every guide is free to read and use on your own site. If you’d rather not do it yourself, or the fix doesn’t hold, that’s when it makes sense to send the site over.

What if I try the steps and the site is still broken?

That usually means the cause is more layered than a single guide can cover — two conflicts stacked on top of each other, or a server-level setting only your host can change. At that point, send me what you tried and what happened, and I’ll take it from there.
